I joined UNICEF’s Young Climate Champions as a participant in August 2024. A year later I came back as an organizer, mentor, and social media manager, and since then I’ve helped run national training camps, LCOY Uzbekistan 2025 (Local Conference of Youth), and RCOY Central Asia 2026 (Regional Conference of Youth).

The story

I joined in August 2024 as one of 50 champions, after a competitive selection. That year, I organized 36 eco-events in my region. After our closing ceremony, the organizers invited me to join their team. I became the only organizer who had started as a participant, and the only one working on all three sides of the program: organizing, mentoring and social media.

In June 2026, during the closing sessions for the next cohort, heavy rain flooded UNICEF’s ground-floor hall. The water was 3 to 5 centimeters deep, and for a moment I just stood there, thinking about the next day’s sessions. Then my supervisor asked me to help. We carried the flags, banners, microphones and participants’ things upstairs, called one venue after another, and set up a new hall at the Avloniy Institute, flags first. Only when the room was ready did I notice I had left my charger in the water. In the end, the closing ceremony went smoothly.

LCOY Uzbekistan 2025

In 2025, we organized Uzbekistan’s first Local Conference of Youth, in Nukus. About 100 delegates, chosen from 1,661 applicants, wrote a National Youth Statement. It was presented to the Senate and at COY20 and COP30 in Belém, and it fed into Uzbekistan’s NDC 3.0. I was the only person on both the organizing team and the statement team. I also helped run a Model Conference of the Parties and gave a talk, “Climate Diplomacy through MUN”.

RCOY Central Asia 2026

In September 2026, we hosted the first Regional Conference of Youth for Central Asia, in Samarkand: 84 delegates from 9 countries. I came as a participant. The night before it started, my supervisor whispered, “We need your help, Shokhruz.” Nothing was final yet: not the agenda, not even the delegates’ return tickets. I took charge of the venue and the flow of the conference. I went to bed around 1 or 2 a.m., woke up at 4, and by 5, I was in the hall straightening the flags, Uzbekistan’s first. When an expert came on stage unprepared, I quietly took him off, saying the plans had changed. On the first day, one of the main organizers put an organizer badge on me, next to my participant badge.

I facilitated the group on governance, climate action plans, NDCs, climate finance and markets. There, I proposed a Central Asian Climate Finance Association: one app where countries post climate problems with a budget, and young people from neighboring countries solve them and get paid. My group supported it, and it went into the statement. When I learned the statement had been drafted before the delegates arrived, I asked the organizers and their seniors to rebuild it from the participants’ ideas, and we edited it until morning. The next day, I skipped the group trip to help the organizers buy delegates’ return tickets, and we finished by lunchtime. In November, I’m taking that statement to COP31 in Antalya as a party delegate for Uzbekistan’s youth.

Eco-Schools Uzbekistan

Eco-Schools Uzbekistan works with the Ministry, FEE, UNICEF, UNDP and FAO. It grew from 22 schools in 2021–22 to 694 in 2025–26, and 209,280 students took part in 2025–26 alone. Its Young Farmer project covers 200 schools, where students have grown 34,000 kg of vegetables, flowers and seedlings. These are program totals. My part: I run the program’s social media, helped organize a training camp for 62 new mentors, and prepare the regional schools data the Ministry sends to the Presidential Administration.

Young Green Ambassadors

With UNDP, Education Above All, Zamin Foundation and the Ministry, I was an organizer and mentor for 70 young people from the 7 regions most affected by climate change. I helped them grow their eco-projects.

Aug 2024 – 2025 · Participant

Cohort 1: becoming a Young Climate Champion

After a competitive selection, I completed the Youth Advocacy Guide training run by UNICEF experts on 21–25 August 2024 and joined the community of 50 Young Climate Champions of Uzbekistan, a project co-organized by the Ministry of Preschool and School Education, UNICEF and Zamin Foundation. Throughout the academic year, we worked to raise climate awareness among young people and children in Uzbekistan. In my region, I organized 36 eco-events and reached 1,463 young people. Across the whole community, we organized 260 eco-events, reached 17,428 young people, planted 3,200 trees, and collected and sorted 13 tons of waste. That year, I also completed emergency-readiness training from the Institute of Civil Protection at the Academy of the Ministry of Emergency Situations of the Republic of Uzbekistan.

2025 – 2026 · Organizer, mentor & social media manager

Cohort 2: from participant to organizer

In the second cohort, I was invited to join the organizing team to help organize and mentor the next Young Climate Champions. This time I worked in the national coordination of the program I had first joined as a participant. I helped organize the Training of Trainers (ToT) and the training camp for 52 new eco champions: planning the sessions, guiding participants through the program, logistics and participants’ safety. I was also a mentor there: I trained new champions in blogging, developed their digital storytelling skills, and taught them how to inspire and reach more people. In addition, I was the social media manager for both the Young Climate Champions and Eco-Schools Uzbekistan, producing videos, reels and posts about climate and youth climate initiatives that reached more than 1,000,000 people and made climate content more visible across the country. I was the only team member working in all three areas, and the only organizer who had started as a participant. In the second cohort, the program reached 17,831 young people and children through 180 events, planted 1,909 trees, collected and sorted 18 tons of waste, and trained 13 mentors. Working in the national coordination gave me real experience in project management.

August 2025 · Nukus, Karakalpakstan

LCOY Uzbekistan 2025: Local Conference of Youth

Together with the Young Climate Champions, we organized the first Local Conference of Youth in Uzbekistan, in 2025 in Nukus, Karakalpakstan. It brought selected young people from all over the country together to write the National Youth Statement, which we then presented to the Senate of Uzbekistan and at COY20 and COP30 in Belém, Brazil, contributing to Uzbekistan’s NDC 3.0 and the Global Youth Statement. Around 100 delegates took part, chosen from 1,661 applicants from all over the country. I was the only team member on both the organizing team and the program and statement team. I was one of the contributors to our National Youth Statement, and I also gave a talk on climate diplomacy, sharing my experience with young people at LCOY.

During LCOY Uzbekistan 2025, we also organized a Model Conference of the Parties (MCOP) to simulate real climate conferences. Delegates negotiated climate solutions and drafted a resolution, strengthening their climate-diplomacy and negotiation skills. The purpose of the MCOP was to let delegates step into the shoes of real diplomats.

November 2025 · Organizer & mentor

Young Green Ambassadors

The Ministry of Preschool and School Education, UNDP, the Education Above All (EAA) Foundation and Zamin Foundation organized the Young Green Ambassadors program together. Seventy young people from the 7 regions of Uzbekistan most affected by climate change took part to develop their climate projects and initiatives. I was an organizer, helping run the program, and a mentor, helping young people grow their eco-events and take them further.

August 2026 · Eco-Schools Uzbekistan

Training the next Eco-Schools mentors

With the Ministry of Preschool and School Education, we organized the Eco-Schools Camp to train 62 new mentors to work with eco-schools effectively. They will go back to their regions, promote the project there and serve as mentors, growing the Eco-Schools network in Uzbekistan. So far, 694 schools have joined Eco-Schools Uzbekistan, and the project has reached 430,000+ students, 11,000+ eco-activists and 1,000+ teachers. It has also held 80+ national events, 9 regional and international conferences, and 50+ online meetings. Under Eco-Schools Uzbekistan, we also run the “Young Farmers” project, which covers 200 schools and 18,000+ students: they have grown 34,000 kg of produce (12,285 kg of cucumbers, 11,858 kg of tomatoes, 4,538 kg of flowers, 4,369 kg of seedlings, 3,584 kg of onions, 2,389 kg of strawberries and 598 kg of other crops), and 200+ teachers have taken part. The Young Climate Champions program is also part of Eco-Schools Uzbekistan.

August 2026 · Organizer

Cohort 3: seeing it from the inside

In the third cohort, I again helped organize the program’s training camp. This time I saw it even more closely, because I was interning at the Ministry of Preschool and School Education. Even though my internship was in the International Relations Department, I helped organize the third cohort so I could understand the process from the inside.

September 2026 · Samarkand

RCOY Central Asia 2026: Regional Conference of Youth

Together with the Young Climate Champions, we organized the first Regional Conference of Youth for Central Asia (RCOY Central Asia) in Samarkand, with 84 delegates from 9 countries. There we developed the Regional Youth Statement, which was then presented to the Senate and to the UN’s Youth Affairs and will be used to write the Global Youth Statement in Antalya, Türkiye. I helped organize the conference on behalf of the Ministry of Preschool and School Education. I was responsible for the flow of the conference (who would speak when, and what would happen next) and for the venue, which I set up for the conference. I also coordinated logistics, helped write the statement, and was a facilitator during the conference and the drafting of the statement.

Since August 2024

Looking back

I joined as one of 50 participants and ran events in my own region. Now I help train the cohorts that come after me. These are the program results since 2024, counted for the whole team:

  • Young Climate Champions: 35,259 youth and children at 440 eco-events, 5,109 trees planted, 31 tons of waste collected and sorted, 89 mentors and 102 new champions trained.
  • Eco-Schools Uzbekistan: 694 schools, 430,000+ students, 11,000+ eco-active students and 1,000+ teachers.
  • My own part: as a participant, I ran 36 events for 1,463 young people in my region. Since then, I’ve completed 2 trainings, helped organize 5 national camps, LCOY Uzbekistan 2025 and RCOY Central Asia 2026, and made content that reached 1,000,000+ people online.

Since I joined the Young Climate Champions community in August 2024, I have been officially volunteering at UNICEF, at the Ministry of Preschool and School Education, and at the Zamin Foundation to raise climate awareness among the youth and children of Uzbekistan.
